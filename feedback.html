Who pays, and why?

Buyer hypothesis: software companies that manage remote inspections need checks at sites that other operators control.

Value to test: replace a manual check or a site visit with a requested device measurement when no reliable integration exists.

No customer or willingness to pay is validated. The test-token price demonstrates the flow, not commercial pricing.

What does FieldProof add?

One request connects the price, payment, device, fresh measurement, and buyer verification.

The buyer commissions a new measurement with its own question and age limit. The operator earns for the check, including an honest unfavorable answer.

A reliable existing API can be enough. Lower integration effort across independent operators remains a hypothesis.

What peaq will do, and the current blocker

Before purchase, the prepared buyer checks the machine's registered identity and signing key on peaq. After delivery, it records the paid-job hash through peaq native Storage.

This makes the provider and its work independently inspectable across applications. Live activation, a new identity-gated purchase, and work-record readback remain pending.

The published Agung EventRegistry points to a different identity registry. The prepared path uses official native Storage and retains the identity checks.

Storage is an operator-controlled record. It does not supply a peaqOS activity event, a credit rating, or proof of honest sensing.

Specific sponsor question: does a registered machine identity plus a receipt-linked native Storage record fit the bounty?

What the evidence proves, and what it does not

A signature connects the reported measurement to the configured device key and original request. It does not prove installation, calibration, physical truth, or completed work.

The prototype measures a contact input. The entrance in the replay is an illustration. No door, delivery, repair, or customer operation connects to it.

Payment buys the check before measurement. No escrow or automatic refund exists. Test purchases are founder activity, not commercial revenue or customer traction.
